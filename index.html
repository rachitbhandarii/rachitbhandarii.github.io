01 · The shift

Give an AI an objective. Let it figure out how.

A CFO doesn't want to program every action. They want to define the business outcome and let an agent work through the details.

“
Get us the best deal for 10,000 units of server racks.
You can spend up to ₹1 crore.
One objective. No predefined sequence of actions.

The agent takes it from here.

1Searches the marketfinds options
2Compares offersevaluates trade-offs
3Negotiateschanges the path
4Makes the decisionchooses ₹87 lakh
5Executes paymentwithout a human click
₹87 lakh is within the limit.

But a spending limit answers whether the agent can pay. It doesn't establish whether this particular payment is what the company intended.

The CFO's problem changes. As autonomy increases, the question is no longer only “was the transaction authorised?” It becomes “did the agent's final action faithfully execute the original objective?”

02 · The protocol

Verify the journey from intent to transaction.

AVS sits at the boundary between an agent's autonomous decision and the movement of enterprise money.

More autonomy

Agents can handle more financial workflows without turning every action into a manual approval.

Less review

Human attention is reserved for ambiguous, high-risk or unverifiable actions.

More speed

Verification happens as part of execution rather than after the money has moved.

04 · Trust at scale

Autonomous does not mean unrestricted.

The point of AVS is not to remove the CFO from the system. It is to make human involvement proportional to uncertainty and risk.

01
Human override

The CFO can pause agents, revoke authority or require approval for selected workflows.

02
Fail safely

If AVS cannot establish a trustworthy intent-to-action relationship, the system does not automatically proceed.

03
Continuous monitoring

Track intent deviation, verification failures, anomalous agent behaviour and policy violations.

04
